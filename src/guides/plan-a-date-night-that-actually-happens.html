Ask most couples why they do not go on more dates and the answer is rarely money or interest. It is the conversation that starts with “what do you want to do?” and ends, forty minutes later, with the television. The problem is not romance. It is logistics. Here is a routine that removes the logistics.

1. Fix the slot, not the plan

Choose a recurring time, such as Thursday evening or Saturday morning, and treat it like any other appointment. A fixed slot means you only decide what, never whether. If one week falls through, the next one is already booked.

2. Take turns owning it

Alternate who plans. The planner chooses and the other person agrees to go along without negotiating. This halves the mental load and doubles the surprise. It also means each of you gets a turn at being looked after.

3. Decide with constraints

Open choice is paralysing. Three constraints make it easy: where (home or out), how much (free, cheap or a treat) and how long. The date generator works exactly this way. Set the three filters, spin, and use the veto sparingly.

4. Add one new thing

Researchers led by Arthur Aron found that couples who did new and mildly challenging activities together reported feeling better about their relationships than couples who did pleasant but familiar ones. You do not need a skydive. A cuisine you have not cooked, a neighbourhood you have not walked, a game you have not played: small novelty is enough.

5. Protect the first twenty minutes

Phones away, no talk about chores, bills or the children for the first twenty minutes. After that, talk about anything. Starting well matters more than the venue. If conversation needs a push, pull one card from a question deck.

The ten-minute fallback

Some weeks the slot will collapse. Keep a fallback that takes ten minutes and no preparation: the daily question, a cup of tea on the step, a walk round the block. A tiny date kept is worth more than a grand one postponed, because it keeps the habit alive.

Tonight

Open your calendars now and block the same ninety minutes for the next four weeks. Decide who plans the first one. That is the whole system.
