Businesses hold weekly meetings because things drift when nobody looks at them. Relationships drift the same way, and usually over small things: a comment that stung, a chore that feels uneven, a week with no real time together. A short, regular check-in catches those while they are still easy to fix.

Set it up

  • Same time every week. Sunday evening works for many couples. Put it in both calendars.
  • Twenty minutes, with a timer. A limit keeps it light and stops it turning into a summit.
  • Comfortable and private. Sofa, tea, phones elsewhere. Not in the car, not in bed.

The five questions

  1. What went well between us this week? Always start here. It sets the tone and reminds you that most of the week was fine.
  2. When did you feel appreciated? Naming it tells your partner exactly what to repeat.
  3. Is anything still on your mind? One item each. Describe what happened and how it felt, without “you always”.
  4. What is coming up that we should plan around? Diaries, money, visitors, deadlines.
  5. What is one thing I can do for you this week? Small and specific.

You will find twenty more in the weekly check-in deck, so the conversation does not go stale.

Three rules that make it work

Listen first. When one person answers, the other only listens and then says back what they heard. Solutions can wait until both feel understood.

One issue at a time. If something bigger surfaces, name it and schedule a separate conversation. The check-in is for maintenance, not renovation.

End on something good. A thank-you, a plan for the weekend, a hug. You should both leave the conversation glad you had it.

When it feels awkward

It will, for the first two or three weeks. That passes. If a check-in regularly turns into an argument, or one of you dreads it, that is useful information, and a qualified couples counsellor can help you find a format that feels safe for both of you.

Tonight

Agree the day and time, set a recurring reminder, and try the first two questions right now. That takes four minutes.
