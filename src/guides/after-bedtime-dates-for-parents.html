Parents of young children are often told to “make time for each other”, usually by people who are not paying for the babysitter. The realistic version is this: the house goes quiet at about eight, you have perhaps ninety minutes before one of you falls asleep, and neither of you wants to plan anything. That is enough. It just needs a different kind of date.

Lower the bar, raise the frequency

One elaborate evening a quarter does less than a small ritual every week. Aim for a date you can start within five minutes of the last bedroom door closing.

Change one thing about the room

The sofa where you fold laundry is not a date venue until something changes. Light a candle, switch off the overhead light, put a blanket on the floor, sit on the step outside. The signal matters more than the setting: this hour is ours.

The first-ten-minutes rule

For the first ten minutes, no talk about the children, the schedule or the house. It is harder than it sounds and it works. If you run dry, pull a card from the ten-minute deck.

Seven dates that fit the window

  • Dessert-only dinner, eaten somewhere other than the kitchen table.
  • A blind taste test with five kinds of chocolate.
  • One episode, one drink, phones in another room.
  • A co-operative video game where you are on the same team.
  • Stargazing from the garden or balcony with a blanket.
  • Ten rounds of Would You Rather.
  • A slow stretch session and an early night.

There are more on the after-bedtime list.

Share the planning

Alternate weeks. The person planning also handles any small preparation, so the other gets to simply arrive. Being looked after for an evening is a large part of what makes it feel like a date.

When you are too tired

Say so, and do the smallest version: tea, the question of the day, bed. Keeping the slot matters more than what fills it.

Tonight

Pick a night this week, put it in both calendars and decide who is planning. Then go to sleep.
